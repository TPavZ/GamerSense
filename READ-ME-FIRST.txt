GamerSense v0.4.20 - smoother category volumes

INSTALL
Stop the old app. Extract the WHOLE App ZIP into a NEW folder and open
GamerSense.exe. Requires the .NET 9 Desktop Runtime, as before.
Source: .NET 9 SDK; BUILD-DEBUG.bat is unchanged.
Saved audio devices, playback mode, export folder and slider values are kept.
First use: all five sliders are 100% (normal). Category routing starts OFF
each time you open the app, even when slider values were remembered.

FIRST TEST
1. Keep your clean Direct cable capture setup: game to CABLE Input;
   GamerSense input CABLE Output; true output Speakers (C6).
2. Start playback. Open Volume controls. Leave Overall at 100%.
3. Check Apply category volumes (preview). Test with Overall 100%, Explosions
   70%, Ground vehicles 70%, Air vehicles 70%, Footsteps 110%.
   For a clearer comparison, adjust just one category at a time. Watch the
   estimated section / briefly holding estimate message.
4. Try Air vehicles, Footsteps and Explosions one at a time. Adjust down
   first; boosts above 100% can clip already loud audio. Clipped-sample
   counts appear in the status/details. 0% mutes a routed section.
5. Uncheck Apply category volumes to compare with normal category volume.
   Overall remains active. Reset all volumes to 100% restores every slider.

WHAT THIS PREVIEW DOES
The latest half-second of ORIGINAL incoming audio is compared to the combined
AC1/AC2 and external-source profiles, separately from playback. Estimates
refresh every 125 ms. A route needs two consecutive fresh estimates, a
clearer separation from the runner-up, and a distance inside a conservative
positive-training range. These are heuristics, not calibrated confidence
or proven unknown rejection. Estimates older than 300 ms and analysis gaps
return smoothly to normal category volume. One ambiguous estimate briefly
   holds the prior stable category (maximum 250 ms), so it does not flip the
   entire mix back and forth. Clear other-category evidence or silence
   releases that route. Gaps and stale input still expire the estimate.

One estimated category controls the WHOLE currently playing mixed section.
For example, if a section is routed as Ground vehicles, footsteps or shots
audible at the same time also change volume. This is not separate stems or
simultaneous source separation. Brief blasts or steps may finish before a
stable estimate arrives. Long engines are a useful first test.
Other categories, including gunfire, reload and building, keep normal
category gain. They still follow Overall volume. Wrong estimates can affect
the wrong section; use the routing-off checkbox for immediate comparison.

Overall and selected category percentages multiply. Category reduction fades
in over 180 ms; recovery/boost fades over 400 ms. Overall changes remain
responsive at 20 ms. Unchecking routing returns category gain to normal in
80 ms, even when recovery was already underway. This adds no output queue
or audio lookahead: playback
does not wait for recognition. At 100% for every slider, output samples are
byte-identical to the original passthrough, with routing on or off.
Processing has not yet been listening-tested on your devices.

CAPTURE / REVIEW
Automatic spikes, retained clips, replay/selection, approve/export and Delete
all pending clips remain available under Captured sounds. Captured WAVs and
analysis use ORIGINAL pre-volume audio. Slider changes never alter training
samples. Pending clips retain one-hour expiry and 2 GB temporary budget.
Approved WAV + label pairs still export to your remembered folder, normally
Documents\GamerSense\ApprovedClips, then leave the pending list.

SPECTRUM REVIEW
All 325 retained sound files were evaluated by native stereo frequency power.
The separate GamerSense-spectrum-review.zip includes category energy profiles,
six exploratory frequency bundles, source-balanced comparisons and a chart.
Vehicles, blasts, footsteps and building sounds share substantial low-band
energy; frequency alone cannot cleanly isolate these categories. No fixed
band cutoffs have been added to live playback.

VALIDATION

cover native unity identity across float and PCM formats; smooth stereo gain,
overall/category/mute, disabled/unknown/stale/gap fallback, volume bounds and
